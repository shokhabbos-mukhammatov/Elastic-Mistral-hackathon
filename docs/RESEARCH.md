# Research: flooding and storm hazards in San Francisco

Verified Nov 3, 2026. 311 statistics are our own analysis of DataSF `vw6y-z8j6` (history) and SF Open311 (live).

## How often and where
From our own analysis of DataSF 311 (`vw6y-z8j6`):
- **About 32,000 flooding reports since 2008.** About 80% are on the street. There are another 85k+ sewage-backup reports.
- **Yearly reports rose from about 945 (2009–13) to about 2,430 (2019–24).** *Caveat: wider 311 app use explains part of this rise.*
- **The pain is packed into a few days.** Since 2019, **27% of all flood reports came in on just 20 storm days**, each with 100 or more reports. The peak was **560 reports on Dec 31, 2022**, when 5.46 in. of rain made it SF's second-wettest day on record.
- **The same places flood every time:**
  - Mission: 2,549 reports
  - Bayview-Hunters Point: 1,808
  - Sunset/Parkside: 1,637
  - SoMa: 1,571
  - Repeat hotspots: 17th & Folsom (the city's lowest point, a former creek) and Cayuga Ave.

## Damage: what these storms have cost
No single official total exists. This is what's on the public record:

| When | What happened | Cost and harm |
|---|---|---|
| Dec 2003 and Feb 2004 | Floods in the Mission, Mission Terrace, Excelsior and SoMa | 40+ property owners sued; the city paid **about $5M** |
| Dec 2014 (two storms) | 17th & Folsom and Cayuga Ave | 255 claims: **$2.1M paid on 155 claims plus $3.5M in related costs**, for more than **$6M** total. Supervisors had to add $5M because SFPUC's claims budget had run out. One café reported about $100k in damage, "the fourth time in six years." |
| Dec 31, 2022 – Jan 2023 atmospheric rivers | 560 flood reports in a day and **3,600+ 311 requests** in that storm, three quarters about flooding. Gilman Ave in Bayview was under about 3 ft of water and the Hunters Point Expressway under 32 in. About 40 cars were ruined in one Folsom St garage. Marina Blvd homeowners filed claims blaming the sewers. | City's initial estimate: **$46M**. That's about $17M in city infrastructure inside SF, at least $25M in city infrastructure outside SF, and about $10M in private property. **117 residents and 17 businesses filed damage reports, and most had no flood insurance.** FEMA declined individual aid, so the only help was SBA loans. |
| Jan 14 and Mar 21, 2023 | Wind on saturated soil. 700 trees and limbs came down on Mar 21, with 662 fallen-tree reports in one day. | **3 people killed by falling trees in SF**: 1 in Golden Gate Park and 2 on Mar 21. Two more were critically injured at Post & Polk. |
| Ongoing | Storms overload the combined sewer, so overflows reach the Bay and the ocean. The dispute (*SF v. EPA*) went to the Supreme Court in Mar 2025. | Legal and environmental cost |
| The fix | SFPUC is spending **$632M on 3 drainage projects**: Wawona, Lower Alemany (about 2028) and Folsom (late 2027). Floodwater Grants go up to $100k. | Years away, and SFPUC says the Folsom area stays at risk after the project. |
| Future | 2024 Civil Grand Jury: **at least 23,700 residents likely harmed by inland flooding**, rain up to 37% heavier by 2100, and **a 1.3 in. / 3-hour storm can already cause flooding**. With 2100 sea level rise: about 31k residents, 168k jobs and 91 miles of streets exposed, worth **$54B to $75B**. | |

*Note: the often-quoted "$6M+ in flood claims" is from the **Dec 2014** storms. The 2022–23 figure is the city's $46M initial estimate.*

## What was missing
1. **The warning existed but didn't turn into action.**
   - NWS issued a Flood Watch on Dec 28, about 96 hours ahead, and sent daily briefings to the city.
   - The city still planned for less than 1 inch (0.5–0.75 in.). It opened its Emergency Operations Center only "late morning" Dec 31, after flooding had started.
   - The mayor blamed NWS, then called it "a misunderstanding."
   - **No one turned a regional forecast into "your block floods at this much rain."**
2. **Protective steps came too late.**
   - The 17th & Folsom flood barriers weren't put up until days after the storm.
   - Sandbags ran out, and the limit was cut to 5 per address.
   - A Mission business owner: "We can't depend on the city."
3. **311 is SF's de facto flood sensor, and it lags and clogs.** From our analysis of storm days vs. normal days, 2019–2026:
   - On Dec 31, 2022, the first flood report came at 5:40 AM, but the 50th didn't arrive until 10:22 AM. By 9 AM about 2.3 in. of rain (Open-Meteo reanalysis) had already fallen. 311 shows flooding only after it's happening.
   - Phone reports jump from **23% to 38%** of the total on storm days, while 911 is telling people to call 311.
   - Median time to close a case is **29.7 hours vs. 21.6 hours** on normal days (37% slower).
   - **About 30% of storm-day flood cases lead to no work:** 14% are duplicates and 16% are "no work needed / nothing found" (vs. 9% on normal days). Crews arrive after the water is gone, or the problem was already reported, and residents can't see existing reports.
4. **There is no real-time street-flood sensing.** NYC runs 250+ public FloodNet sensors. We found no public equivalent in SF.
5. **Risk information is static and hard to find.**
   - The 100-yr map is static and covers stormwater only. Most people see it only in a sale or lease disclosure.
   - The Grand Jury's recommendation #5: **"better inform the public about flood insurance options and expected flood areas."**
6. **There's an insurance gap.**
   - Homeowner and renter policies exclude flooding, and most 2022 damage reporters were uninsured.
   - NFIP flood insurance has a 30-day waiting period, so it has to be bought before the storm.
   - The city pays claims only for inadequate drainage, and it argues that big storms exceed any system.
7. **Language and access gaps.**
   - AlertSF sign-up is documented in English and Spanish, and alerts aren't block-specific.
   - Unhoused residents couldn't find shelter information without a phone or internet.
   - The Healthy Streets Operation Center's website said calls wouldn't be returned.
8. **Ownership is fragmented.** The Grand Jury found the Port and SFPUC directors met together only once in the years it reviewed. Storm coordination is "case by case," and there is no list of resilience projects or budget line for them.
9. **Maintenance can't keep up.** There are 25,000 catch basins. Crews deep-clean only 5,000–9,000 a year, about 3,300 are adopted by volunteers, and SFPUC watches about 700 hotspot basins.

## Who gets hurt most
- Mission and Bayview residents, including Bayview as an environmental-justice community
- Unhoused people
- Seniors and people with disabilities
- Limited-English households (DPH's Flood Health Vulnerability Index `cne3-h93g` scores English proficiency)
- People who walk, bike or ride Muni (Muni Metro was suspended by flooding at Van Ness and in the Twin Peaks tunnel; cars were submerged under US-101 at Cesar Chavez)
- Small shops on flood-prone blocks

Also relevant:
- **Live right now:** an NWS Coastal Flood Advisory runs Nov 5–9, 2026, with up to 1.6 ft of inundation.
- **Climate trend:** the increase in extreme rain comes almost entirely from atmospheric rivers (Scripps 2019), with 20–30% more moisture transport projected (UCLA).

## From gaps to requirements
| Gap | What the product must do | Feature |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Forecast not turned into impact | Convert rain, tide and alerts into a block-level risk level, hours ahead | Block Briefing and risk engine; the replay backtest **measures** the lead time |
| 2. Actions too late | Send specific actions while there's still time | Alerts like "move your car; sandbags at Kansas & Marin until 2 PM" |
| 3. 311 lags and duplicates | Show what's already reported; make new reports precise | Live 311 layer with an "already reported" view; photo to 311 draft with exact location and category |
| 4. No sensors | Combine the signals we do have | Rain, tide, 311, CHP and street signals, each with a confidence level |
| 5. Static map | Make the map personal and live | Address to flood zone, sea-level-rise zone and storm history |
| 6. Insurance gap | Reach people before the season | Pre-season brief: NFIP's 30-day wait, Floodwater Grant, sandbags |
| 7. Language and access | Any language, by text or by voice | Mistral multilingual, iMessage/SMS, Voxtral voice |
| 8–9. Fragmented ownership, maintenance | Help target scarce effort | Adopt-a-Drain nudges on high-risk blocks; a city-ops view |

**Impact pitch:** we don't claim to stop floods. We **close the gap between "the forecast knew" and "the resident acted."** That means:
- earlier block-level warnings
- fewer wasted 311 trips
- more drains cleared before storms
- help programs explained in every language

It complements AlertSF and NWS; it doesn't replace them. It ports to any US city with 311 and NWS data.
